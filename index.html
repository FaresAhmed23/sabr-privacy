Privacy Policy for Sabr

Last updated: 15 October 2026

Sabr ("the app") is an Android screen-time blocker published by Fares Ahmed ("we", "us"). This policy explains what the app accesses, what it does with it, and what leaves your device.

The short version: Sabr has no account, no analytics, no advertising, and no server of its own. Your app lists, your deed history, and your settings stay on your phone and are never sent to us. The one thing that leaves your device is ordinary DNS lookups, which must reach a DNS resolver for the internet to work — and only for websites you have not blocked.

1. What Sabr accesses on your device

DataHow it is accessedWhy
App usage statistics — which app is in the foreground and for how long Android's Usage Access permission (PACKAGE_USAGE_STATS) This is the core of the blocker: detecting when you open an app you asked Sabr to guard, and enforcing daily time limits.
Installed apps — the names and packages of apps with a launcher icon Android package manager So you can choose which apps to restrict. Icons are read per app, only to display them in that list.
DNS lookups — the domain names your apps and browser request Android's VpnService API To match requests against the blocklist and block them before they connect.
Your blocklists — chosen apps, chosen websites, schedule and limit settings Created by you, stored on device To enforce your settings.
Your activity in Sabr — deeds completed, breaks earned, streaks Created by you, stored on device To show your progress and calculate streaks.

Sabr does not access your messages, contacts, photos, files, calendar, microphone, camera, location, or the contents of your network traffic. It cannot read the pages you visit, only the domain names your device looks up.

2. What leaves your device

Nothing is sent to us. Sabr has no backend, no account system, and no telemetry. We never receive your app lists, your usage history, your domains, or your deed history.

Two things do leave your device during normal operation:

1. DNS lookups for websites you have not blocked. To load a site, its domain must be resolved by a DNS resolver. Sabr intercepts lookups so it can block the ones on your list, and forwards all other lookups to a resolver — either the resolver configured by your network (Wi-Fi/mobile), or a public resolver such as Cloudflare (1.1.1.1) or Google (8.8.8.8) as a fallback. On this path requests are sent unencrypted, so the resolver can see the domain names being looked up. This is the same exposure as browsing without Sabr, except the request passes through Sabr's local filter first. Lookups for domains on your blocklist are never forwarded — they are answered locally with a null address. Sabr does not blackhole or intercept any traffic beyond DNS.

2. Your in-app purchases, if any. Sabr is distributed free and charges nothing. If that ever changes, payment processing would be handled by Google Play, under Google's privacy policy, and this policy would be updated before release.

3. What is stored, and where

All of it is on your device:

  • App-level storage (Android AsyncStorage): your blocked sites, blocked apps, deeds, break sessions, daily stats, and settings.
  • Service-level storage (Android SharedPreferences): the same enforcement config, kept by the blocking service so it can keep working after the app is closed or the phone restarts.
  • A cache directory for app icons, which Android may reclaim at any time.

None of this is backed up or synced — Sabr sets allowBackup=false, so your data is not copied to Google's cloud backup either.

4. Permissions, and why each is needed

  • Usage access — detect the foreground app and enforce limits (the blocker).
  • Display over other apps — show the pause screen on top of a blocked app.
  • VPN (VpnService) — run the local DNS filter. See the disclosure Sabr shows in-app before this is switched on.
  • Notifications — tell you when a break is ending, and show the ongoing "Sabr is active" notification required for the service.
  • Run after restart — resume blocking after a reboot.
  • Alarms — schedule break-expiry and break-warning notifications.
  • Battery optimisation exemption (optional) — ask you to exempt Sabr so aggressive battery managers don't stop the blocker.

5. Sharing and disclosure

We do not sell, rent, share, or transfer your data to anyone. There is no ad network, no analytics provider, and no crash-reporting SDK in the app. The only third party that sees anything as a result of using Sabr is the DNS resolver described in section 2, because the DNS protocol requires a resolver to answer a lookup at all.

We would only disclose data if legally compelled, and because we hold none, we would have nothing to disclose.

6. Diagnostics you choose to send

Sabr's Settings → Diagnostics screen can show a technical snapshot (device model, Android version, permission states, and whether the guard service is running) and offers a Share button so you can send it to us when reporting a problem. This only happens if you tap Share and pick a destination yourself.

That snapshot deliberately excludes the raw record of which apps you opened — package names are reduced to short non-reversible tags and timestamps are stripped before anything is shareable. The same rule applies to the system log: the app does not write the package name of the app you opened, or the domain you attempted to visit, to logcat. Nothing is ever sent automatically.

7. Children

Sabr is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. Because the app collects nothing on our side, there is nothing for a child's data to be exposed to. Parents looking for a family screen-time tool should be aware that Sabr is designed for an individual managing their own device.

8. Your control and deletion

You are in control of everything Sabr stores:

  • Reset all data — Settings → Reset All Data deletes your blocked apps, blocked sites, deeds, stats, settings, and resets the native enforcement config.
  • Clear the site list — turning website blocking off removes the blocklist from the VPN and stops the tunnel.
  • Uninstall — removing the app deletes all app storage, the service preferences, and the icon cache.

Since we hold no data about you, there is no server-side record to request or delete.

9. Security

Data stays in your device's private app storage, which other apps cannot read. Sabr's VPN service is declared with BIND_VPN_SERVICE and is not exported, so other apps cannot drive it. Internal broadcast receivers are not exported. The app disables Android cloud backup. Note the honest caveat from section 2: the DNS forwarding path itself is unencrypted, which is inherent to serving as a local DNS filter for sites you have not blocked.

10. Changes to this policy

If Sabr ever adds a feature that sends data anywhere — analytics, sync, crash reporting — this policy will be updated and the change disclosed before that feature ships.
